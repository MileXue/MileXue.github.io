Private Equity Replication & Desmoothing

Can listed equities replicate private-equity exposure—and how much risk does appraisal smoothing conceal?

Fit excess returns to five equity factors (36 observations), apply a first-order desmoothing correction, then compare risk and simulated one-year portfolio outcomes. The fitted exposures suggest strong public-equity sensitivity and a growth tilt. R² describes the full five-factor model, not the market factor alone.

Research takeaway

Smooth reported returns can obscure risk. A liquid replication strategy still needs to be understood through its public-equity exposures.

Source: Project 2 exported factor_model.csv, risk_comparison.csv and scenarios.csv. Research illustration: the project documentation identifies the private-equity benchmark as placeholder data; these outputs are not verified live fund performance.

Seed-Stage Deal Review · Anonymized case

Strong early traction is only the start. Does the evidence support the growth plan, entry price, and ownership required?

01 · Traction02 · Valuation03 · OwnershipPass
Evidence lens / Traction

Separate live revenue from ambition.

Live ARR→Committed ARR→Forecast ARR

The review distinguishes revenue already live from customer commitments and the future growth target. A large shop pipeline supports the opportunity, but conversion and higher revenue per shop still have to materialize.

What would change the view?

Evidence that the pipeline converts into paying shops and that expansion revenue supports the planned ramp.

Investment judgment / Pass

Early traction and product fit were promising. The ambitious revenue ramp, demanding entry valuation, and difficulty securing the required ownership and board position outweighed the opportunity.

Based on the completed Project 3 deal review. Company and investor identities, transaction figures, and source documents are withheld; this view presents the analytical framework and the review's conclusion.
